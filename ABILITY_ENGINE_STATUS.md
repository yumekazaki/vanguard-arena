# Ability Engine Implementation Status

## Existing architecture

The website is a static GitHub Pages frontend. `app.js` owns browser-side match state, UI, card parsing, and effect resolution. Online rooms relay actions through Supabase Realtime (with PeerJS for older rooms); the host browser applies both players' actions and sends snapshots. There is no authoritative match server in this repository. Supabase SQL handles room discovery and deck/account data, not game action validation.

The tracked card catalog contains 14,022 records from a community Cardfight Area snapshot dated 2025-05-09. It is not an official Bushiroad API. The current checkout uses sparse checkout, so tracked `assets/` and `supabase/` files are not materialized locally.

## Implemented foundation

- `ability-engine.js` separates ability parsing and event helpers from the UI. It preserves printed text and card definition IDs, parses AUTO/ACT/CONT labels, basic circle headers, once-per-turn text, and GB thresholds.
- Match state has a match ID and event sequence. Human drive, damage, and generic trigger checks and practice-bot drive checks now dispatch structured check events carrying the acting player, checker, exact revealed card, battle ID, check index, check type, and revealed trigger. Local, online setup, and subsequent practice/online turn transitions dispatch a `turn.started` event with the active player identity; conservative AUTO parsing distinguishes the controller turn from the opponent turn. End-of-turn AUTO windows now queue before turn cleanup and transition, and pending player decisions hold that transition until resolved; controller-turn and opponent-turn wording are matched separately. No turn-start or turn-end effects are marked supported without reviewed definitions. Check-event-specific card trigger templates and effects are not yet reviewed or supported. Card instances have distinct UIDs and separate owner/controller IDs. The instance constructor now accepts a structured initial state for zone/circle, orientation, face state, rested state, modifiers, statuses, and ability-use records; the current UI still stores some of this state in player zone arrays and match-level fields, so this is not yet a single authoritative model. Structured events carry actor/turn player, source and subject instance IDs, controller/owner, locations, circle, battle/timing data, and immutable serializable before/after snapshots where supplied. Processed events and event/card/ability trigger claims are deduplicated.
- Trigger matching checks the exact card instance and required circle for supported attack, boost, ride, placement, hit, draw, stand, and movement events. Supported movement paths emit source/destination details, including manual Rear-Guard swaps, field-to-hand/deck/G-zone transfers, Order-zone transfers, and human/practice-bot Guardian Circle placement and departure; exact returned-to-hand/deck triggers are recognized and instance-matched. Unknown triggers, conditions, and costs fail closed rather than entering the automatic prompt queue.
- Host and joining-player pending decisions are routed by captured controller identity. Guest setup decisions, actions, and selections carry match, actor, request, and state-version values; the shared request validator rejects stale, duplicate, unsupported command, and wrong-actor requests; ability decisions also require an explicit accept/decline and correctly sized unique Counter-Blast, Soul-Blast, and discard selections, which the resolver then revalidates against current zones, and the host confirms a selected instance belongs to the joining player. Relay messages labeled as watcher traffic cannot enter the player action path. Player relay now uses separate anonymous Supabase auth sessions, authenticated room-seat claims, private guest-to-host and host-to-guest channels, and database RLS that binds channel read/send access to the claimed seat. Each client subscribes to the incoming seat channel and publishes on its own outgoing channel. This protects player-relay identity and direction, but does not make game actions authoritative: the host browser still validates and applies accepted commands. Stride cost selection follows the player who chose to Stride. Joiner-only Stride and Perfect Guard cost prompts now travel in private, owner-projected state and render in the joiner’s client; canceling Stride sends a state-changing request, and watcher snapshots omit both pending cost prompts. Relay players and official spectator clients now use separate broadcast topics, so the official watcher path receives only public snapshots. This still is not a rules-authoritative server: authenticated seats prevent ordinary clients from impersonating the opposite seat on the player relay, but the host browser remains the match reducer.
- Manual activation is limited to reviewed ACT effects and checks player control, turn/Main timing, circle, conditions, costs, once-per-turn use, and resolution windows. AUTO abilities are queued from events; CONT abilities are evaluated separately and are not presented as manual activations. Mandatory and optional prompt paths are distinguished, and same-controller simultaneous AUTO abilities from one event or one grouped stand phase can be ordered by the player.
- Reviewed effect programs validate all steps before applying them, stage costs and effects atomically, roll back a rejected step, and defer generated events until commit. Ability cost payment now lives in the shared engine, validates unique exact card selections and source location before mutation, pays Counter-Blast/Soul-Blast/discard/source-to-Soul/rest costs, records once-per-turn use, and emits structured movement events. Implemented typed handlers include draw, damage-zone placement without a damage check, power/critical/Drive modifiers, target power changes, continuous power, Counter-Charge, Soul Charge, deck reorder, retirement, rest, and stand. Turn-scoped modifiers expire for their recorded controller.

A current catalog audit found 17,240 AUTO/ACT/CONT ability blocks across 14,022 records: 11,581 AUTO, 2,249 ACT, and 3,410 CONT. The working engine compiles 3,509 blocks into structured definitions across 145 exact-pattern families; 13,731 blocks remain unsupported or unreviewed. This is 20.35% by exact text match, not full card-pool coverage. The audit reports zero invalid compiled definitions, but that check does not prove each definition works in every real game state. Compiled definitions still need representative resolution and multiplayer tests, and unknown or unreviewed definitions remain unavailable rather than guessed. The full Node suite passes 537/537 tests. Current counters are recorded in `catalog-progress.json` from the October 26, 2026 audit.

The engine covers exact subject matching for supported attack, boost, placement, ride, hit, draw, stood, and movement events. It includes reviewed effect examples for draw, selected deck position, power modifiers, opponent Rear-Guard retirement, stand, common costs, and a continuous Soul condition. Many card effects and movement actions remain unsupported or unaudited.

The latest batch adds exact ride-event matching for nine cards whose optional Soul call is conditioned on a same-clan rider that is not one named card. ### Latest implementation increment (2026-10-26)

Drive and damage checks now keep the exact revealed card in a public trigger zone while its trigger effect and resulting AUTO abilities resolve, then move it to hand or damage as appropriate. Over Triggers are removed from the fight; when revealed by damage check they also draw a card, give one of the checking player's units +100,000,000 power, and do not enter the damage zone. Remaining damage checks wait until those trigger effects and choices resolve. Six-damage loss is checked after pending trigger effects (so a Heal trigger can change the outcome). Standard trigger effects include power/critical, draw, heal, front-row power, stand, and Over handling. Stand trigger restrictions prevent that unit from performing drive checks for the turn. Battle resolution now rechecks the attacker and target, resolves hit or block after drive-trigger changes, retires a hit Rear-Guard, and waits for close-step AUTO abilities before ending the battle. Trigger choices are owner-routed. Some multi-part trigger choices and full official timing remain unsupported.

5. Add browser-driven two-client tests for host/joiner decisions, privacy, consistent state, reconnect, retries, and concurrent activations.
